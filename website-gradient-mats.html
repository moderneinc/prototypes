Gradient mats

Colour comes back to the page as a bed under a screenshot, not as a border on a card. Every gradient here is composed from the five spectral M-FISH stops already in tokens.css — nothing new is introduced. This is the same palette as the retired spectral heading gradient, mixed heavily toward the page ground so it reads as tinted paper rather than neon.

Where the colour comes from

The spectral gradient was retired as a hairline ring around cards and as an animated conic border. The palette itself was never the problem — the application was. These mats reuse the same five stops at a scale where a gradient actually works.

Two definitions of the ramp are in circulation

The design kit ships a six-stop ramp — pink, violet, blue, teal #25D0C0, green, gold. The website's tokens.css ships five: the teal is not there.

The teal is clearly present in the product screenshots this page is modelled on, so the samples here use the kit's six. That gap needs closing in one direction or the other before any of this ships — and the teal has no deep/day companion, so it has no legible form on the cream ground yet.

The one-line recipe

color-mix(in srgb, var(--spectral-blue) 22%, var(--bg))

Stops stay tokens; only the mix ratio changes per surface. 22% on the cream ground, 34% on the dark one — the dark ground needs more stop to register. Retune the whole system from one number.

Full-bleed bands

The same mixes as a band background, with copy on top rather than a screenshot. At the tinted ratios the ink ladder still holds; at full strength it does not, and the type has to go light.

Tinted band, normal ink

Text keeps --bone and --bone-dim. This is the safe option and should be the default for any band carrying real copy.

Radial pools, normal ink

More movement than a linear ramp, same contrast behaviour, because the stops never get strong enough to fight the text.

Full spectral, light type

Only where the band is a moment rather than a place to read — a hero, a campaign header, a closing frame. Contrast has to be checked per stop, not assumed.

How to use them

Under artwork, not around it
The mat is a bed for a screenshot or a figure. It is not a card border, a hairline ring or an animated edge — those are what got retired.
One mat per view
Two gradient beds in one viewport start competing. If a page has several screenshots, give one the mat and let the rest sit on the plain ground.
Never set body copy directly on a strong mix
At the tinted ratio the ink ladder holds. At full strength it does not — that band needs light type and a contrast check per stop.
The screenshot keeps its own ground
Panel fill plus a hairline, so the gradient never shows through the artwork. Same rule as the figure plates: lighter than the page, and not white.
Stops stay tokens
Compose with color-mix against --spectral-*. A hardcoded hex cannot follow the --warm dial between grounds.
No animation
Static only. The rotating conic border is the thing this replaces.
